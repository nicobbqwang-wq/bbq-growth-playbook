Independent consultants: “easier to follow” and “better business results” are different claims.

Consider this fictional example, supplied as a synthetic evaluation fixture—not real customer results:

In Cedar Research’s May 2025 pilot, 12 volunteers tested a checklist. Seven said it was easier to follow. There was no control group, and no conversion or revenue tracking.

That supports a narrow observation about those volunteers’ feedback. It doesn’t establish business impact. This was Cedar Research’s fictional pilot, not my project.

When evaluating a checklist for your consulting work, separate three questions:

- Can readers follow the steps?
- Can they complete the intended task?
- Does completing it affect a business outcome?

Each question needs its own evidence.

My proposed next step is to test the checklist with consenting readers. I haven’t done that yet.

Before testing your next client resource, decide which question you want it to answer—and what you’ll measure.
