Independent consultants: an easier-to-follow checklist isn’t necessarily a better-converting one.

Consider this synthetic example—not real customer evidence: in a fictional Cedar Research pilot, 12 volunteers tested a checklist in May 2025. Seven said it was easier to follow.

That tells us about reported ease of use. With no control group and no conversion or revenue tracking, it doesn’t establish business impact.

Before testing a checklist, decide what you want to learn. Can readers follow the steps? Where do they get stuck? What would you need to measure separately to assess business outcomes?

My proposed next step is to test the checklist with consenting readers. I haven’t run that test yet, and Cedar Research’s fictional pilot was not my project.

The practical takeaway: match the claim to what you actually measured.
