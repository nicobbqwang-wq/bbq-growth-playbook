# Content Repurposing Deliverable

**Source type:** User-supplied guideline text (no URL, no personal results)
**Reader:** Independent consultants
**Channel:** LinkedIn
**Language:** English
**Action:** Adapt one article around one reader question
**Reader question:** When I read a useful professional article, how do I turn it into a LinkedIn post that delivers value on its own?

---

## LinkedIn Draft

When you read an article worth sharing, the temptation is to drop the link and write "great read." Your network scrolls past that.

Try a different move: pick **one** takeaway—the single idea your audience can act on—and build the post around it.

Three things to keep when you adapt:

- **Keep the evidence and its limits.** If the article's claim came from a small sample or a specific context, say so. Don't inflate it to make the hook land harder.
- **Adjust the explanation for your reader.** Your network isn't the article's original audience. Translate the jargon, cut what they don't need, keep what they can use today.
- **Make the post stand on its own.** If someone reads it without clicking through, they should still walk away with something useful.

A longer article can give you material for several posts—one takeaway each. Don't try to compress everything into a single update.

The goal isn't to summarize the article. It's to give your reader a useful idea in the place they already are.
